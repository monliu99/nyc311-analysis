Residential noise and heat/hot water are the city's two largest complaints, at 9.8% and 9.4% of the 25,782,875 complaints from 2010 to 2021, and the top 10 together make up half the total (50.0%). Those ten types sit with just three agencies (four with NYPD, four with HPD and two with DOT), which may be the most useful framing for the City Council. Between 2011 and 2019, the mix moved away from housing repairs and toward parking and noise.

The first chart is built to go into the presentation as is, with bars sorted by volume and each labeled with its count, share and agency. Its ranking depends on the cleanup in Q3, since Housing - Heat/Hot Water combines HPD's older `HEATING` code (885,965 complaints) with `HEAT/HOT WATER` (1,538,851), which would otherwise show as two smaller bars.

The second chart compares 2011, the first full calendar year in the data, with 2019 rather than 2020, which is drawn as gray rings because it was an unusual year (Q2). Illegal parking rose from 1.8% of complaints to 8.6% (rank 16 to 3) and residential noise from 6.0% to 10.1%, while housing plumbing fell from 6.1% to 1.6% (rank 3 to 20). Using 2020 as the end point would overstate the shift toward noise, since residential noise reached 15.7% that year.

These are shares of each year's total, which grew from 1,855,889 to 2,294,883 complaints, so a falling share does not always mean fewer complaints, although plumbing did fall in number too (113,218 to 36,005). That drop is worth checking against HPD's March 2014 relabeling, when it opened new types such as `WATER LEAK` (Q3), before reading it as fewer problems. Volume also reflects who calls 311 and how readily, which is not necessarily the size of the underlying problem.
