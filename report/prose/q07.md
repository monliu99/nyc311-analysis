Complaints per resident vary 3.4-fold across the 59 community districts, and the three insights on the map below come down to one story, which is that the heaviest residential 311 use sits in lower-income districts of the Bronx, upper Manhattan and Brooklyn, and that housing complaints about heat, hot water and building conditions explain most of the difference. The measure throughout is complaints per 1,000 residents a year (243 citywide), which puts a small district and a large one on the same footing.

Midtown (Manhattan 5) tops the list at 472, but largely because of how the rate is built, since relatively few people live there and many more commute in or visit, so complaints about construction noise, taxis and broken parking meters (its top types in the Q8 dashboard) are divided by a small number of residents; only 7% of its complaints are about housing. The next rows tell the residential story better, with Manhattan 12 (Washington Heights, Inwood) at 405 and Bronx 5, 7 and 4 at about 355, where housing is 45% to 57% of complaints.

The second insight's "64% of the gap" is easiest to read as a what-if, since the 10 highest-rate districts file 358 complaints per 1,000 residents a year against 167 in the 10 lowest (a gap of 191), and housing complaints make up about 155 against 33 of those, which means that if both groups filed housing complaints at the same rate, about two-thirds of the gap would go away. Noise accounts for most of the rest (34%), and all other categories add under 3% between them. The third insight ties this to income, as the poorest fifth of the 55 income areas (Census areas that closely match districts) files nearly five times as many housing complaints per resident as the richest fifth (123 against 26 per 1,000 a year), while other complaints run slightly the other way; the rank correlation with income (a score from −1 to +1 for how consistently two rankings move together) is −0.78 for housing complaints and +0.30 for everything else.

These figures measure reporting rather than need, and housing complaints are tenants reporting landlords to HPD, so districts with more renters will likely file more of them and a low rate is no proof of good conditions. In addition, 4.2% of records (mostly Department of Transportation requests without a usable address) have no district, income comes from 2010–2014 Census estimates, and a pattern across areas says nothing about individual households.
