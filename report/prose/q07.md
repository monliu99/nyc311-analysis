Complaints per resident vary 2.9-fold across the city's residential districts, from 404 per 1,000 residents a year in Manhattan 12 (Washington Heights, Inwood) to 139 in Manhattan 8 (Upper East Side). The three insights in the map below come down to one story, which is that the heaviest 311 use sits in lower-income districts of the Bronx, upper Manhattan and Brooklyn, and that housing complaints (heat, hot water and building conditions) drive most of the difference.

Midtown (Manhattan 5) is left out of the rankings, a judgment call, because it has few residents (58.5k) and many commuters. Its 470 complaints per 1,000 residents (only 7% about housing) say little about where people live, and counting it would stretch the spread to 3.4-fold.

The second insight is easiest to read as a what-if, since the 10 highest-rate districts file 349 complaints per 1,000 residents a year against 167 in the 10 lowest, and housing makes up about 161 against 33 of those. If both groups filed housing complaints at the same rate, 70% of the gap would go away (the category shares below sum past 100% because smaller categories run the other way). The third ties this to income, as the poorest fifth of the 55 income areas files 123 housing complaints per 1,000 residents a year against 26 in the richest fifth, nearly five times as many, while other complaints run slightly the other way (155 against 176).

These rates measure reporting rather than need, and housing complaints are tenants reporting landlords to HPD, so districts with more renters likely file more of them and a low rate is no proof of good conditions. In addition, 4.2% of records (mostly Department of Transportation requests) have no district, and income comes from 2010-2014 Census estimates.
