Unresolved complaints pile up most in Staten Island, home to three of the top four districts, rather than in the city's lowest-income districts, mostly because of the kinds of complaints filed there. Compared like for like (same complaint types and years), there is no clear link between an area's income and how long its complaints take to close or how many stay open.

Unresolved means an open status (such as Open or Pending) with no close date, true of 1.9% of complaints. About a fifth of open complaints citywide, though, were opened in February-June 2011 and are likely old records never closed rather than live problems. Three in four complaints (75.4%) are about people and businesses and 16.8% the city's own infrastructure, but natural complaints (weather, trees and animals), just 5.1%, make up 40% of those still open. They also account for about four in five of the 17,415 open complaints in Staten Island 3 (Tottenville), the most of any district.

"No clear link" means that the rank correlations with income (a −1 to +1 score of how closely two rankings move together) are small, −0.11 for time to close and −0.23 for the share left open, and could plausibly be chance. Per resident, the poorest fifth of areas has 4.3 unresolved complaints per 1,000 residents a year against 5.2 in the richest. A straight-line fit across districts does find a small tilt (about 2.7% faster closing for each doubling of income), but it fades to almost nothing within boroughs. The clearest outlier is Wakefield (Bronx 12), whose complaints take 35% longer to close than typical for its mix (Q4), likely worth a direct look.

These are associations across areas, and income travels with renters, housing stock and density, so none of this shows what drives service (and closing a ticket is not fixing the problem).
