New Yorkers filed 25,782,875 complaints with 311 between January 2010 and November 2021, and the numbers point to three things the Mayor should know about where demand is heading, where the burden falls and where service is falling behind (the files are partly missing from September 23, 2021 on, so daily figures stop on September 22).

### Three critical findings

1. **Demand is growing and changing shape.** Complaints rose 23.7% from 2011 to 2019 (1.86 to 2.29 million a year) and reached 2.59 million in 2020, while illegal parking went from 1.8% to 8.6% of complaints by 2019 and residential noise jumped from 10.1% to 15.7% in the first pandemic year.
2. **The housing burden falls on lower-income neighborhoods.** The poorest fifth of areas file 123 housing complaints (heat, hot water and building conditions) per 1,000 residents a year against 26 in the richest fifth, and housing accounts for 70% of the gap between the 10 highest- and 10 lowest-rate districts, half of the top ten being in the Bronx.
3. **Slow service is mostly about what is being fixed, and the backlog sits in a few places.** Complaint type explains more of the variation in closure times than agency does (68.6% against 57.1%), and once complaints are compared with others of the same type and year there is no clear link between an area's income and how fast its complaints close or how many stay open; complaints with natural causes (trees, weather, animals), however, are 5.1% of complaints but 40% of those still open, with Parks leaving 16.0% open and the Health Department 17.2% against 0.1% at the NYPD.

### Two immediate actions

1. **Clean up the open backlog, starting at Parks and the Health Department.** Many open tickets are likely records that were never closed rather than problems still waiting, since 106,792 of them (22% of all open complaints) were opened between February and June 2011, so a one-time review, oldest first, would make the open count meaningful again and show which complaints truly need a crew, with Staten Island a sensible place to start (Staten Island 3 has the highest share still open, 4.6%, and about four in five of its 17,415 open complaints have natural causes).
2. **Staff ahead of the weather and the calendar.** Heat complaints run almost ten times higher on nights below 20°F than on nights in the 50s (+890%), and sewer and flooding complaints more than double on heavy-rain days (+133%), so HPD and DEP can consider adding crews when the forecast calls for either, and 311 can schedule from the same weekday over the last four weeks adjusted for holidays, which misses daily volume by about 8.6% a week ahead.

### One long-term recommendation

**Measure whether problems get fixed, and judge agencies and districts like for like.** The data record when a ticket closes rather than when anyone responds or whether the problem was fixed, and raw averages mostly reflect each agency's or district's complaint mix, which means that a dashboard built on them can reward the wrong things; over the next year or two, the city should consider recording first response and a confirmed fix for its largest complaint types, reporting medians against the typical time for the same type and year, and pairing 311 with independent measures of need (such as HPD violations), so that the housing burden in lower-income districts is tracked as an outcome and not only as a count of calls.
