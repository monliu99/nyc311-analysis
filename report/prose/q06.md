Weather lines up with what New Yorkers complain about far more than with how many complaints come in. Compared with a night in the 50s in the same month and on the same weekday (holidays accounted for), a night below 20°F goes with about 35% more complaints in total, but almost ten times as many heat and hot water complaints (+890%). Heavy rain of half an inch or more goes with 133% more sewer and flooding complaints. For the Office of Emergency Management, that likely means planning around a few weather-sensitive complaint groups rather than the citywide total.

Content shifts just as clearly, as heat and hot water make up 34% of complaints on nights below 20°F and about 1% on nights of 70°F or more. Some effects arrive a day late, with sewer complaints still 40% higher the day after heavy rain, tree complaints 36% higher (likely as people find the damage afterwards) and snow and ice complaints 509% higher the day after an inch or more of snow.

For staffing, the seasonal chart at the end is likely the most usable piece, as heat complaints in a typical year average 1,508 a day in January against 73 in August. The typical year averages 2011-2019, a judgment call that keeps the pandemic years apart, since 2020 broke the pattern (noise in June ran 186% above a typical June).

These are associations, not proof that weather causes complaints, since weather also changes who is outside to notice a problem (noise complaints fall 20% on heavy-rain days). In addition, one Central Park station stands in for all five boroughs, and a few extreme days, such as August 4, 2020, with 15,690 tree complaints, are drawn at the top of capped axes in the first chart.
