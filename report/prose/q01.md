Most of the 41 columns have some missing values (33 as stored, 36 once placeholder text such as `Unspecified` counts as missing), but the fields the Mayor's Office relies on most are complete. The request number, opening date, agency and complaint type are filled in on all 28,240,000 rows, so counts by type, agency and date can be taken largely at face value. The gaps that matter are in location, where 1.6% of requests still have no borough and 5.1% no community district after cleaning.

The table below gives three counts, since blank cells alone both understate and overstate the problem. The park facility field is never blank but says `Unspecified` on 99.3% of rows. In the other direction, 13 columns are empty on at least half the rows mostly because they don't apply; the intersection street fields, for example, are 74.1% blank overall but 1.3% on intersection addresses.

Recording has improved since 2010, when 34.0% of requests had no borough as recorded (0.6% in 2021). The one field moving the wrong way is address type, missing on 3.8% of requests in 2010 but 54.5% in 2020, which is likely worth raising with the 311 team.

Location gaps are also uneven by agency (only 78.1% of DOT and 80.9% of DOF requests can be placed in a community district, against 99.4% for NYPD), so the district views later in this report likely undercount street and finance complaints. And *where it applies* rests on a rule of thumb (a column applies to a complaint type that fills it at least 5% of the time), which can flip for the 202 of 478 types with fewer than 100 rows.
