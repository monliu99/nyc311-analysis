The largest numbers of unresolved complaints are in Staten Island and parts of Queens, not in the city's lowest-income districts, and that is mostly a matter of what those districts complain about (trees, streets and other public infrastructure, which stay open longer). Once each district is compared with the citywide record for the same complaint types and years, lower-income districts do fare somewhat worse (a rank correlation of about −0.3 with income for both time to close and the share still open), which is worth being ready for in a debate but well short of evidence that the city serves poorer neighborhoods worse.

Unresolved here means a status other than Closed or Cancelled (Open, Pending, Assigned and so on), which applies to 2.5% of complaints citywide, and the most sit in Staten Island 3 (Tottenville, Woodrow, Great Kills), with 22,399 or 5.9% of its complaints. Compared with what each district's complaint mix would predict, the Bronx stands out instead, as Bronx 10 (Throgs Neck, Co-op City, Pelham Bay) has 14,696 complaints still open against 11,449 expected.

Some complaint types are strongly concentrated, with consumer and licensing complaints 7.8 times as common in Midtown as citywide (the location quotient below) and housing-condition complaints about twice as common in Bronx 5. By origin, using the groups from Q3, three in four complaints (75.5%) are about the actions of people and businesses, 16.7% about the city's own infrastructure and services, and 5.0% natural (mostly trees). City complaints stay open more often (3.8%, against 1.5% for people and businesses) and make up a larger share in wealthier districts (rank correlation +0.68 with income), which is largely why those districts show a higher raw share still open (+0.52), even though like for like the tilt runs the other way.

The like-for-like comparison is the fair one for the equity question, and it shows a modest tilt, as districts with lower incomes take somewhat longer to close the same kinds of complaints (−0.28) and have somewhat more still open beyond their mix (−0.30). The size is small, though, at about 3% faster closing for each doubling of income, and both links largely disappear when districts are compared only with others in their own borough, which means that the pattern is more a difference between boroughs than between neighborhoods.
