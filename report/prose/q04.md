The data has no first-response time, so this section measures time to closure, the time from when a complaint is opened to when the agency marks it closed. A typical complaint closes in 30.3 hours (the median), while the average is 22.3 days because a small share stays open for months or years, so the Mayor's dashboard should report medians rather than averages. Compared like for like, most neighborhoods differ only slightly, with one clear exception in Bronx 12 (Wakefield, Williamsbridge), where complaints take 36% longer than typical, and most of what looks like one agency outperforming another is really the mix of work each one handles.

Two groups of records bend the statistics, as 975,814 complaints (3.9%) show no time at all between opening and closing, most of them (872,490) likely administrative closures, and 777,292 (3.0%) are still open and so missing from every figure, which likely makes the closed-only numbers look faster than reality. The trend also needs care before it goes into a speech; the median fell from 3.7 days in 2010 to 3.4 hours in 2021, but for the same complaint types in the same borough, 2021 closures took 22% less time than 2010's, and on that same basis 2019 was still 7% slower than 2010, which suggests that much of the headline drop comes from a shift toward fast-closing complaints such as noise (see Q5) and that the like-for-like gain is recent.

On neighborhoods, raw medians point to three Bronx districts (Bronx 2, 5 and 6, each at 3.0 days), but much of that reflects the slower kinds of complaints they receive. The chart below is mix-adjusted, meaning each complaint is compared with the citywide typical time for the same complaint type and year; on that basis the slowest districts are Bronx 12 (+36%), Queens 1 (Astoria, Long Island City, +14%) and Queens 2 (Sunnyside, Woodside, +11%), and the fastest is Manhattan 1 (Battery Park City, Tribeca, −14%). The two rankings have almost nothing in common (a rank correlation of −0.01), and the adjusted list is the better basis for a promise of improvement, since residents there wait longer for the same kind of complaint.

On agencies, the Mayor's hunch is largely right, as complaint type alone explains 68% of the variation in closure times against 57% for agency alone, and adding agency on top of type, year and borough adds only 0.6 percentage points (second chart below). Only 10 of 20 agencies handle a complaint type that another agency also handles (those shared types are 3.6% of closures), so most agencies can only be judged against their own history and targets, and even where two agencies share work, as with Derelict Vehicle (a median 2.9 hours at NYPD against 24.0 at DSNY), the gap may reflect different work, such as closing by referral, rather than better service. Throughout, closure records when a ticket was closed rather than whether the problem was fixed, and these comparisons are associations, not proof of better or worse service.
