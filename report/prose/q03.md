As recorded, the complaint type field is not reliable enough to budget on, but a reviewed lookup table fixes that, and this analysis built one that maps 478 raw values to 298 kinds of work in 17 categories. The problems are not cosmetic; when HPD renamed `HEATING` to `HEAT/HOT WATER` in March 2014, a raw count would show 885,965 heat complaints ending and 1,538,851 new ones beginning. Budgets set on raw type counts would move with naming decisions rather than with demand.

The field also mixes spellings of the same thing (`PAINT/PLASTER` and `PAINT - PLASTER`) and two naming systems, with HPD's ALL-CAPS codes (17.7% of rows) next to everyone else's Title Case. Some entries are not complaints at all, as 72 types (8.7% of rows) are requests for information or services and 113 raw types (130 rows) are junk, mostly web-form probes.

The proposed strategy keeps the raw value and adds a standard type, category, origin (used in Q9) and information-request flag from a lookup table with a note for every merge, which Council analysts can review without reading code. Three levels keep the detail operations need (17 categories, 298 types and the 1,850 descriptors as they are). Names merge only after checking agency and descriptors, so NYPD's `Derelict Vehicle` and its successor `Abandoned Vehicle` become *Abandoned Vehicle (with plates)*, while DSNY's look-alike `Derelict Vehicles`, a different service with no shared descriptor, stays apart as *Derelict Vehicle (no plates)*. New spellings are matched by exact name, then a tidied name, then a fuzzy match flagged for review, and leftovers are listed for a reviewer to add as one row, with no code change.

Some mappings are judgment calls (such as whether a sidewalk or rodent complaint stems from the city or from residents), recorded in the map's notes but best confirmed with the agencies. Type-level trends across HPD's March 2014 and DSNY's September 2021 reorganizations also need care, while category-level trends are on firmer ground.
