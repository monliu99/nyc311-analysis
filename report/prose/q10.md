The most valuable features for predicting daily complaint volume are recent volume and the day of the week, combined as the average of the last four same weekdays (for a Tuesday, the four previous Tuesdays). That baseline missed the daily count by 9.3% on average one week ahead in 2017-2019, and adding holidays, month and weather brought it only to 8.4%.

Each model learned from the three years before a test year and was scored on that year's unseen days by its average miss, as a share of the actual count. A feature's value is how much it cuts that miss, pooled over 2017-2019 (a judgment call to keep the pandemic years, shown separately, out of the ranking).

Matching the weekday cuts the miss by 3.2 points, likely because agencies and many callers keep a work week, and recent counts cut it by 1.7 points, since they carry the current level of demand. Holidays cut another 0.7 points and helped in all three years, while month and weather show no reliable gain, likely because the last four weeks already carry the season. In 2020 demand jumped, so a model without recent counts missed by 23.1% while recent counts were worth 10.7 points, and the weekly rhythm faded (weekends reached 103% of weekday volume, against 84% in 2019).

For response times, compared with the typical time for the same complaint type and year, the same features matter much less. Friday's complaints take 17.5% longer than Monday's, other features held fixed (likely because the weekend falls in between), and on the same basis a day's own volume shows no link, so busy days don't appear to slow closures. Complaints opened after March 22, 2020 closed 24.3% faster than earlier ones, relative to their type's usual time, a single event the data can flag but not explain; all of these are correlations, not causes.
