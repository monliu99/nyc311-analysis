The dashboard below is a single web page that opens in any browser, with a map of all 59 community districts that staff can zoom into, hover over for a district's rate and rank, and click for a short profile. It covers 24,213,298 complaints from 2010 to September 22, 2021, shaded by complaints per 1,000 residents a year (Q7's measure), and at street level it shows individual complaints from the last 30 complete days.

For a council office preparing for a board meeting, the click is likely the most useful part, since the profile lists the district's top five complaint types, how quickly its complaints close, the share still unresolved and each year's rate. The buttons at the bottom right switch the shading to one of the four largest categories, such as noise or housing conditions.

Time to close is shown two ways, and the mix-adjusted one (compared with the citywide typical time for the same complaint types and years) is the fairer read. Midtown (Manhattan 5) is a good example, as its median complaint takes 2.0 days (about 48 hours) to close against 41.3 hours across all 59 districts, which looks slow, yet it closes the same kinds of complaints 8% faster than typical.

Rates are per resident, so Midtown and other districts with many workers look high (see Q7), and the dots are a random sample of 20,000 of the 263,364 located complaints in that 30-day window (8%), so cluster numbers count sampled complaints. Recently opened complaints don't inflate the unresolved share, as 1.2% of those from the last 30 days are unresolved against 2.0% for earlier days. The map stops on September 22, 2021, because the last 59 days in the data are partly missing, and staying current would mean rerunning the pipeline on each new extract.
