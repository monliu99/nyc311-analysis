The complaint type field is not reliable enough to budget on as recorded, but it can be made reliable with a reviewed lookup table, which is what this analysis built. The 478 raw values describe 297 kinds of work in 17 categories, and the inconsistencies are more than cosmetic; HPD's `HEATING` stops on Mar 10, 2014 and `HEAT/HOT WATER` starts the next day, so a naive count would show 885,965 heat complaints ending and 1,538,851 new ones beginning, and DSNY retired 15 types and introduced 16 in September 2021. Budgets set on raw type counts would therefore move with naming decisions rather than with demand.

Beyond renames, the field mixes spellings of the same thing (`PAINT/PLASTER` and `PAINT - PLASTER`, both HPD), look-alikes that are in fact different work (HPD's `PLUMBING` and DOB's `Plumbing` share none of their 22 descriptors) and two naming systems, with HPD's ALL-CAPS codes (17.7% of rows) next to everyone else's Title Case. Some entries are not complaints at all, as 72 types (8.7% of rows) are requests for information or services, such as bulky item pickups, and 113 raw types (130 rows) are junk, mostly web-form probes from a single day in June 2019.

The proposed strategy never overwrites the raw value and instead adds a standard type, a category, an origin (the city, residents, nature or other, used in Q9) and an information-request flag from a lookup table with a note for every merge, which Council analysts can review without reading code. Three levels keep the detail operations need (17 categories for budgeting, 297 types for agency planning, and the 1,850 descriptors as they are), and names are merged only after checking the agency and descriptors, so look-alikes that are different work stay apart. New spellings in future data are matched by exact name, then by a tidied name (case, spacing, plurals), then by a close fuzzy match flagged for review, and anything left over is listed by row count for a reviewer to add as one row, with no code change; on this data none of the 478 types needed review.

Some of the mapping rests on judgment calls (whether a sidewalk or rodent complaint stems from the city or from residents, for instance), which are recorded in the map's notes but are best confirmed with the agencies. In addition, 65 retired types with at least 1,000 rows have no clear successor, and HPD's `GENERAL CONSTRUCTION` was split across several new types in 2014, so type-level trends across 2014 and late 2021 should be read with some care, while category-level trends are on firmer ground.
