Residential noise and heat/hot water are nearly tied as the city's two largest complaints, at 9.8% and 9.4% of all complaints from 2010 to 2021, and the top 10 together make up half of the total (50.0%). Those ten types sit with just three agencies (four with NYPD, four with HPD and two with DOT), which may be the most useful framing for the City Council, and over the decade the mix has moved away from housing conditions and toward noise and parking.

The first chart below is designed to go into the presentation as is, with bars sorted by volume and each labeled with its count, share and agency. Its ranking depends on the cleanup in Q3, since Housing - Heat/Hot Water combines HPD's older `HEATING` code (885,965 complaints) with `HEAT/HOT WATER` (1,538,851), which would otherwise appear as two smaller bars.

The second chart compares 2010 with 2020, the first and last full calendar years in the data, and shows residential noise rising from 6.0% of complaints to 15.7% and street noise from 0.9% to 8.0%, while heat/hot water fell from 11.1% to 6.4%. These are shares of each year's total, which grew from 1,910,092 to 2,593,899 complaints, so a falling share does not always mean fewer complaints, although heat/hot water did fall in number too (from 212,314 to 165,016).

The main caution is that 2020 was an unusual year in the data (311 requests from June to October ran 39% above the same months a year earlier, led by noise, as Q2 shows), so the 2010 versus 2020 comparison likely overstates the long-run shift toward noise. Volume also reflects who calls 311 and how readily they do so, which is not necessarily the size of the underlying problem.
