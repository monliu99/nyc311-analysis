Data collection has been broadly consistent from Jan 1, 2010 to Nov 20, 2021, with no empty days and a typical day of 6,497 requests, but it has three breaks. The last 59 days and four days in January 2010 are likely incomplete because batch files are missing. Several agencies also changed how or whether they record, and closing dates are much less reliable than openings.

Most spikes and dips are real events rather than collection failures, since 80 of the 145 unusually low days are federal holidays and the high days usually have a clear cause. On Aug 4, 2020, 311 logged 24,415 requests against a usual 9,668, and Damaged Tree complaints made up 97% of the excess. The recording changes are quieter, as DOF, HRA and NYCEM stop in June 2019 and HPD logged opening dates without a time of day until 2015.

Closing dates are weaker, since 261,538 requests were closed before they were opened (7.53% of DOT's closed requests) and 1,044,470 at the very second they were opened, likely administrative closures rather than work done. On Sep 10, 2019, DSNY closed 18,470 requests against a usual 811, 95% of them at the same midnight timestamp, which looks like a backlog clean-up rather than a day's work.

The second chart averages the full calendar years 2011 to 2019 and draws 2020 and 2021 apart, because their busiest weeks ran 120% and 82% above the typical level. Openings peak on Tuesdays (7,016 a day) and closings on Thursdays (7,182), while closings drop more on weekends (3,804 on Sundays against 4,655 openings), which means that agencies fall behind over the weekend and catch up later in the week. The weekday matters more than the season, as the typical year ranges only from 5,265 a day in week 52 to 6,983 in late January. Both views show when requests are logged, which is not always when problems occur.
