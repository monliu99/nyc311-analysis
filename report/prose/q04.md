The data has no first-response time, so this section measures time to closure. A typical complaint closes in 30.3 hours (the median of all closed complaints), but the average is 22.3 days because a few stay open for months or years, so the dashboard should report medians. Like for like, one neighborhood stands out, Bronx 12 (Wakefield, Williamsbridge), and agency differences mostly reflect the work each one handles rather than better service.

Two groups of records bend these numbers, starting with 872,490 complaints closed at the second they opened. These are likely administrative and are left out below, which raises the median to 40.7 hours. Another 493,441 (1.9%) are still open, which likely makes closed-only figures look faster. The trend also needs care before it goes into a speech, since the median fell from 3.7 days in 2011 to 23.3 hours in 2019 while the same types in the same borough took 7% longer. That points to a shift toward fast-closing NYPD complaints such as noise and parking (Q5).

Raw medians point to the Bronx, but that mostly reflects slower kinds of complaints. On the mix-adjusted chart below, which compares each complaint with the citywide typical time for the same type and year, Bronx 12 (+35%), Queens 1 (+14%) and Queens 13 (+12%) are slowest. In Bronx 12, residential noise accounts for 71% of the gap, with a median of 3.5 hours against 1.8 citywide. In five of the six districts at either end, the largest driver is a complaint NYPD handles (noise, parking or abandoned vehicles), so precinct response to these calls is likely where a promise of faster service would be felt most.

On agencies, the Mayor's hunch is largely right, since complaint type alone explains 69% of the variation in closure times against 57% for agency, and adding agency to type, year and borough adds only 0.3 points (second chart below). Only 10 of 20 agencies share a complaint type with another, and in just 3 of those 7 types do both log the same kind of request, so for most agencies *exemplary* can only mean fast against their own history or targets. Closure also marks when a ticket was closed, not whether the problem was fixed, so none of this proves better or worse service.
