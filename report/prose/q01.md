For the uses the Mayor's Office cares about most, the 311 data is more trustworthy than its empty cells suggest, since the request number, opening date, agency and complaint type are filled in on all 28,240,000 rows, so counts by type, agency and date can be taken largely at face value. The weak spot is location; 4.5% of requests have no usable borough and 10.5% no community district as recorded, although cleaning recovers many of them from the coordinates and ZIP code and leaves 1.6% and 5.1% unplaced.

How much is missing depends on how it is counted, so the table below shows three versions. Counting only blanks, 33 of the 41 columns have gaps, but the city often writes *missing* as text (the park facility field is `Unspecified` on 99.3% of rows and the intake channel `UNKNOWN` on 21.1%), which raises the count to 36. In the other direction, 13 columns are empty on at least half the rows mostly because they apply to only a few complaint types (the intersection street fields are 74.1% blank overall but 1.3% on intersection addresses), which the *where it applies* column corrects for.

Recording has also improved a lot since 2010; in that year 34.0% of requests had no borough as recorded, 14.2% had no coordinates and 32.7% had no time of day on the opening date, and by 2021 those figures were 0.6%, 2.4% and 0.1%. The one field moving the wrong way is address type, missing on 3.8% of requests in 2010 but on 54.5% in 2020 and 41.2% in 2021, which is likely worth raising with the 311 team.

Two limits remain, the first being that location gaps are uneven across agencies (only 78.1% of DOT requests and 80.9% of DOF requests can be placed in a community district, against 99.4% for NYPD), so the district views later in this report somewhat undercount street and finance complaints. The second is that *where it applies* rests on a rule of thumb (a column applies to a complaint type that fills it in at least 5% of the time), which can flip for the 202 of 478 types with fewer than 100 rows.
